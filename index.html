Plastic Pollution Is Not Just an Ocean Problem

Plastic waste often starts on land and is transported through drains, rivers and lakes before reaching coastal waters and the ocean, where it affects aquatic ecosystems and wildlife.

LAND → DRAINS → RIVERS → LAKES → OCEANS → AQUATIC LIFE

How Plastic Harms Aquatic Animals

Ingestion

Animals can mistake plastic for food, causing gut blockage, injury, reduced nutrition, starvation and sometimes death.

Entanglement

Discarded nets, ropes and lines can trap animals, restrict movement and interfere with breathing or feeding.

Habitat Damage

Debris and abandoned fishing gear physically damage aquatic habitats and breeding grounds.

Reduced Feeding / Nutrition

Plastic-filled stomachs reduce real food intake and weaken growth and survival potential.

Injury and Death

Sharp fragments and long-term exposure can cause wounds, infection, and fatal stress responses.

Food-Web Transfer

Plastic particles can move through aquatic food webs from small organisms to larger predators.

Evidence basis: UNEP, NOAA Marine Debris Program, FAO and peer-reviewed marine ecology studies.

Microplastics: The Invisible Threat

Larger plastic materials fragment into smaller particles (microplastics), which can be ingested by plankton and other small organisms, then pass through fish and predator food webs.

Plastic Pollution in India

Key India Indicators

  • 4,136,188.83 tonnes (2022–23) — Annual plastic waste generation reported to CPCB. Source: CPCB Annual Report 2022–23 (as reported in PIB response)
  • 1 July 2022 — Nationwide prohibition on identified single-use plastic items. Source: Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2021; CPCB implementation notices
  • Major river concern: Ganga basin — High population and waste pressure make river systems critical for intervention. Source: CPCB/State pollution control reports, National Mission for Clean Ganga context

What Can We Do?

1. REDUCE

Cut unnecessary single-use plastic consumption at source.

2. REUSE

Use durable alternatives and extend product life cycles.

3. RECYCLE

Improve source segregation and channel recoverable plastics into recycling streams.

4. BETTER WASTE MANAGEMENT

Strengthen collection, material recovery and safe disposal infrastructure.

5. PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY

Enforce EPR so producers manage packaging waste and improve product design.

Policy focus: India’s Plastic Waste Management Rules (2016, amendments) and prohibition of identified single-use plastic items from 1 July 2022.

Why Should We Care?

Plastic pollution is tightly linked to aquatic ecosystems, wildlife survival, food-web stability, human activity patterns, waste-management systems and sustainable development goals.
